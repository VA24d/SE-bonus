Towards Energy-Aware Design Choices in
Event-Driven Microservices
Vijay – 2023111007

As software systems increasingly transition toward cloud-native microservices and event-driven architectures (EDA),
decisions regarding service topology, message broker selection, and data serialization formats are traditionally guided
by scalability, resilience, and responsiveness. However, these structural choices profoundly influence CPU cycles,
network traffic, memory pressure, and message broker overhead—metrics that correlate with physical hardware energy
consumption. For organizations deploying cloud-native distributed systems at scale, minor inefficiencies in per-event
processing compound into significant environmental and infrastructure costs.

The motivation for this research is to evaluate software design patterns for microservices through a sustainability lens,
recognizing that macro-level architectural choices often exert a significant long-term impact on resource utilization.
This project presents a simulation study to determine whether common event-driven design decisions can be
systematically ranked by their resource consumption per business event using proxy metrics.

The experimental scope evaluates three critical dimensions through controlled simulation and empirical benchmarking:

1. EDA Topology: Chain versus Parallel Fan-Out. Empirical measurements show that parallel fan-out topologies
reduce end-to-end latency by 66.1% compared to sequential chains, improving CPU utilization density.

2. Broker Choice: Apache Kafka versus RabbitMQ. Mathematical modeling indicates that RabbitMQ is more
efficient for low-volume deployments (<2,034 msg/s), while Kafka's linear CPU scaling provides better
per-message efficiency at enterprise scale.

3. Serialization Format: JSON versus Protobuf. Compiled Protocol Buffers reduce payload size by 77.5% and
CPU parsing time by 27.7% compared to JSON, with TDP-estimated energy reduction of 15.8%.

The workload simulates realistic asynchronous business workflows using Python-based benchmarks. Measured metrics
include latency per event, payload size, CPU time, and TDP-estimated energy consumption. These proxy metrics
provide guidance for resource-efficient software architecture design, with future work planned to validate findings
through physical hardware energy telemetry on a containerized testbed.
